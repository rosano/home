Back in Canada to do administrative things, I must confront choices about detaching: I'm comforted by the supports and amenities available to me in my family home, but I sense the city's culture makes me cynical and frustrated over the long-term.

I might also end up on a longer than expected break from my capoeira group and movement training to sort myself out, which brings me to the arena of *self-practice*. Recent attempts to train kicks and handstands in the company of a chair have made me realize how much I've relied on group trainings to avoid conscious organizing and development—a sort of autopilot. Even though music instilled in me an understanding of how advantageous it is to practice between lessons, I chose to skip that with movement earlier this year to focus on projects and just keep my body active with the group. Starting again has clarified a couple of broader reflections:

1. Individual practice without the intensity of group training may seem less "intense" or "focused" as you find your way, but it also takes extra energy to self-organize and adapt to make sessions possible in environments that are fluctuating, noisy, less private, not purpose-built, or adverse for other reasons.
2. Physical training, daily routines, and food choices can be spaces to practice building your path based on [having a memory of what you did](https://rosano.ca/strolling/0183/), which is a reflex in musical composition and improvisation.
3. Body connection *is* our desired "magnificent integration of all the things", with electricity built-in.
4. The cost of being sedentary for long periods is often invisible, and forces bringing us there fuel this silent decay.

The administrative puzzle means I will be looking for work over the next year, partly to support myself, partly to prove in visa applications that I am worthy of existing in a new place. Although I've been focused on my own projects since 2020, I feel a hunger to be in service of those in my community; my capacity to encircle problems by creating friendly technical solutions, combined with my bent for communications, can meaningfully help an absurd number of people. Someone recently asked me how I ended up on my path, and it reminded me that both projects floating me financially for a decade were oriented towards serving: [AudioScrub](https://rosano.ca/audioscrub) gave musicians new repertoire and skills; [note the sound](https://rosano.ca/notethesound) freed up venue owners to spend time on their business while their event promotion was handled well. I had fun making some square business cards for people who already know me as a way to share how I can help; only showing it in person 🙂.

### [Brian Christian: The Most Human Human](/log/series/brian-christian-the-most-human-human/)

> [People ask "how did you meet your spouse?" and not "how did you stay married?" because the latter is a story of struggle and pain.]

> [Conversation is best as duet over duel: each positioning the other to say great things, playing for the game and assist rather than the score.]

> [Advice from someone who responds less predictably has more value because of entropy.]

> ["Impersonal friends" are those we are drawn to for their _type_, which makes them more or less fungible, in contrast to specific individuals we care about which can't be swapped. The difference develops through shared history.]

> each new step towards AI, rather than producing something which everyone agrees is real intelligence, merely reveals what real intelligence is _not_.

> [Template interactions like "Je voudrais un hot dog, s'il vous plaît … merci!"; "Où est le WC? … merci!" are simple to use, but have no cultural or experiential value, and treat the other as a machine. If someone gives a surprising or interesting response, you might miss it.] Wielding language's magic is intoxicating; becoming susceptible to it, even more so.

> [People are always changing as you get to know them—partly due to your presence; they are never still.]

> [Neurons change their connection structure each time they fire. Functional brains change. Experience changes you or it didn't happen. Relating is risky because you can't get to know someone without changing them or somewhat becoming them.]

> [If a shape is repeated enough times at a small enough scale, it becomes texture.]

> [Geometric relationships between canvas and content help keep the viewers eye on the composition.]

> [Use direction and organize elements into explicit and implicit lines to guide the eye from one section to the other.]

> [We don't see pure colours. They are spectrums that our visual system approximates, which results in our experiencing of the dominant color.]

> [Shopping in a Target store involves being assaulted with red in every direction, and there is a joke that you cannot leave without spending $100.]

### [Marshal McLuhan: Understanding Media](/log/series/marshal-mcluhan-understanding-media/)

> [A society without games is trapped in automation.]

> [In indigenous society, there is no true art because everybody is engaged in making art.]

> [Electric technology extends the nervous system and enables direct immediate communication between neurons of everyone on the planet.]

> [With TV, the viewer is the screen: millions of dots of light go through them and a small subset are accepted in order to create the images.]

> [Studying media's effects might baffle those who never studied it, as with people in pre-literate cultures who might say "Why do you write? Can't you remember?".]

### [The ABCs of How We Learn](/log/series/daniel-schwartz-jessica-tsang-kristen-blair-the-abcs-of-how-we-learn/)

> [Ineffective readers take no time to interpret the text: they merely paraphrase or re-read and are learning instead of learning _from_.]

> [How does this fit what I know and what I have read so far?]

> [Productive agency cycle in five steps: 1) make; 2) see the fruits of your labour; 3) share it; 4) overcome new challenges; 5) learn strategies and methods.]

> [Having students work in groups to report an answer can help them feel less personally threatened when confronting negative feedback.]

> [Complex questions bind information to a network of ideas rather than isolated facts. This improves memory by creating a multitude of connections between ideas.]

> [Efficient knowledge can be accessed quickly and applied to recurring contexts, but it can impede seeing all possibilities when prior knowledge no longer applies.]

> [A leash gives both the dog and master an illusion of freedom.]

> [Money has expanded much faster than the value it represents.]

> [There can be no recipes for revolutionary organization because it is anti-recipe.]

> [The leverage of reading and writing comes from the words of the literate and not from the educator's experience.]

> [Studying means to create and recreate, rather than repeating what someone else said.]

> [Swimming, one learns to swim. Working, one learns to work. Practicing, one learns to practice better.]
